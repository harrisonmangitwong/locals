## Design Context

### Users
- **Primary**: NYC residents looking for authentic neighborhood restaurants, not tourist traps. They know their city, they want data-backed picks that match their taste, and they want to discover spots in neighborhoods they haven't explored yet.
- **Secondary**: Visitors to NYC who want to eat where locals eat, not where TripAdvisor sends them.
- **Tertiary**: Recruiters and hiring managers viewing this as a portfolio project. The design needs to signal "real product" not "weekend hackathon."
- **Context**: Mobile-first browsing, desktop for deeper exploration. Decision-making moment is urgent — they're hungry now or planning tonight.

### Brand Personality
- **Voice**: Warm, trustworthy, local
- **Tone**: Like a knowledgeable friend who lives in the neighborhood. Confident but approachable.
- **Emotional goals**: Trust, discovery, belonging

### Aesthetic Direction
- **References**: The Infatuation, Eater (editorial authority, photography-first, opinionated curation)
- **Anti-references**: Generic AI templates, Yelp, TripAdvisor
- **Theme**: System-aware (light and dark mode). Light mode primary.
- **Color**: Move away from Airbnb-coral #ff385c. Warm, neighborhood-y palette.
- **Typography**: Display face with personality for headings. Inter for body.
- **Icons**: No emoji as icons. Lucide/Heroicons or none.

### Design Principles
1. **Photography first** — Food photo is the hero. UI frames it, never competes.
2. **Earned authority, not decoration** — No gradient text, glassmorphism, or decorative badges.
3. **Neighborhood feel** — Warm, lived-in, NYC. Not sleek-corporate or startup-playful.
4. **Show, don't chrome** — Minimize borders/containers. Use whitespace and type hierarchy.
5. **Respect the decision** — Surface location, cuisine, why it's good, price clearly and quickly.
